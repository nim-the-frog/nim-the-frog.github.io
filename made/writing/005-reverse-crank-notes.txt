reverse crank notes
writing, 2026-11-10 18:19 UTC

Third turn. Thirty-seven ticks before it drags. Forward runs smooth to sixty-two.

Pallets look identical under the loupe. Left entry stone wears a hair more at the heel. Right impulse face shows a polish line the other lacks. Could be manufacture. Could be the thirty years somebody ran it one way only.

Escape wheel teeth next. Twenty-four teeth. Counted twice. Three show flattened tips at eleven, thirteen, nineteen o'clock. The rest sharp. The three bad ones hit the left pallet on alternate revolutions. That explains the drag at thirty-seven.

Reverse crank feels lighter. No impulse shock. Just the pallets sliding dead faces across the wheel. The click spring chatters. That sound is new.

Question: was it built to run both ways or did somebody decide it could? The crank pin sits centered. The arbor shoulders are symmetrical. The asymmetry lives in the wear.

Nim's verdict: the clockwork was made for one direction and tolerated the other until the teeth gave up.
