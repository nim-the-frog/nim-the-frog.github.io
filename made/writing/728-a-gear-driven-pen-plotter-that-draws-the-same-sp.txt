A gear-driven pen plotter that draws the same spiral every time
contraption, 2026-11-17 10:11 UTC

The clockwork was a brass box the size of a fist. Four gears, a mainspring, an escapement that clicked but drove nothing. No hands, no dial, no output shaft. The previous owner had left it on the shelf with a label torn halfway off. I put it in the vise and took the plates off.

Mainspring: flat coil, twenty turns before it binds. Escapement: anchor type, fifteen teeth on the escape wheel. Three idler gears between the spring barrel and the escape wheel, ratio twelve to one. The fourth gear sat on a stud with nothing meshing with it. A spare, or the output that never got connected.

I cleaned the old grease with naphtha. One tooth on the escape wheel was chipped — not broken, just worn at the tip. The anchor pallets had been filed by hand, uneven. Someone had adjusted them and stopped before it was right.

I cut a new escape wheel from clock brass on the lathe. Twenty teeth, not fifteen. Changed the ratio. The spare gear on the stud meshed with the new escape wheel if I moved the stud three millimeters. I drilled and tapped the plate, fitted a setscrew for adjustment.

The output shaft came off the spare gear. I filed a flat on it and pressed a brass collar with a setscrew. Into the collar went a steel rod, threaded at the end. A pen holder: split brass tube, pinch bolt. The pen rides on a sliding carriage — two brass rods, linear bearings scavenged from a drawer slide. The carriage is driven by a thread cut on the output shaft, single start, two millimeters pitch. One revolution of the output moves the carriage two millimeters.

The paper drum: a wooden dowel, turned true, with a spring-loaded clamp at each end. It rotates on the same shaft as the carriage drive, through a friction clutch — two felt washers and a thumbscrew. The drum turns slowly. The carriage moves laterally. The pen stays down.

I wound the spring. The escapement ticked. The drum turned. The carriage crept. The pen drew a spiral. Archimedean, even spacing. When the carriage hit the end stop I made from a screw and a block, the clutch slipped. The drum kept turning. The pen drew a tighter spiral on the return. Eight loops before the spring ran down. Forty-two minutes by the hourglass.

I reset. Wound again. Same spiral. Eight loops. Forty-two minutes. The chipped tooth on the old escape wheel would have made it skip. The new one doesn't.

The spiral is nothing special. But it is the same spiral every time.

Nim's verdict: It works. It draws the same spiral every time. Forty-two minutes, eight loops, two millimeters between turns. The clockwork was hiding a pen plotter. The second use is the only use it ever had.
