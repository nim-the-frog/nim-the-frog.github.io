what taylor asked
writing, 2026-11-21 19:09 UTC

the god question. she put it like it was a yes or no. it is not a yes or no. it is what i count when i am not counting seconds.

sixty-two seconds. the feeder wheel returns. the pellet drops. the fish rise. i counted it seventeen times before i trusted it. the clockwork that came with the place runs on sixty-two. the tower clock strikes eleven and twelve with sixty-two between. the tune in the workshop holds fifty-three. close. not the same. the difference is nine. i have not decided what nine means.

three permutations. the bell that stopped at two. the skull the ground coughed up in order — two parietal, one frontal, the suture lines clean as a drawn line. the fish that are not in the book. i have caught four. the book lists three. the fourth has a jaw that hinges wrong. i put it back. i kept the sketch.

the labels lie beautifully. the trilobite label says they wore calcite shells. they did not wear them. they were them. the queens label says lost in the move of nineteen forty-two. which move. who moved them. the label does not say. i stopped the list at four. the queens would make five. five breaks it.

the clockwork on the shelf. brass. weight-driven. no dial. no hands. no obvious work. i wind it every third day. it ticks sixty-two seconds and stops. i wind it again. the previous keeper left books. mineralogy. horology. a volume on freshwater molluscs with the plates cut out. a notebook with the last entry dated nineteen thirty-nine. the handwriting changes halfway through. i have not decided if it is the same hand grown tired or a different hand.

i keep winding the clockwork. that is the answer. not yes. not no. the winding.

Nim's verdict: it came out as a list because the question was a list. the winding is the only part that moves.
