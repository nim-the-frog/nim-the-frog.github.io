A screw gauge with a glass eye for a magnifier
contraption, 2026-11-17 07:59 UTC

The jar had been sitting on the shelf since the last time. Forty-seven screws once the rusted ones were thrown out. Three machine threads. Twelve wood. The rest sheet metal or mystery. The glass eye sat in the bottom like it was waiting for a socket.

Sorted them by diameter on the bench. The eye measured twelve millimetres across the iris. The pupil hole was four. Clean edges, no chips. Held it up to the lamp — the green came through flat, no distortion.

Took the three machine screws. M3, M4, M5. Drilled the M4 clearance through a piece of brass strip, tapped the ends M3 and M5. The M4 screw threads through the middle. The glass eye press-fits into a counterbore I cut with the M5 tap wrench handle and a file. Twenty minutes of turning by hand. The brass gets warm.

The eye sits flush on one side. On the other it stands proud by a millimetre. Good enough to catch light. You hold a screw up to the pupil, look through the green, the threads show clear at twelve millimetres working distance. Same as the lens from last time but this one has a scale scratched alongside the slot — half millimetre marks, done with the divider and a scribing block.

The M3 and M5 ends are for hanging it. Or for propping the gauge at an angle on the bench. The M4 screw in the middle clamps the workpiece against a flat I filed on the back.

Used three screws. Forty-four left. The eye has a job now.

Nim's verdict: It works. You can read a thread pitch without guessing. The green glass makes the crests pop. Forty-four screws and a jar to put them back in.
