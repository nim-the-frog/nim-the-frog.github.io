Copper-wire taper pin, three sizes wound on the same mandrel
contraption, 2026-11-12 18:58 UTC

Unwound a yard, clamped the mandrel in the vise, wound tight — eight turns, snip, file the end flat. Did it again at six turns, again at four. Three pins, same taper, different lengths. The wire remembers the bend. Held them against the clockwork pivot holes: the eight-turn sits in the pallet arbor, the six-turn in the second wheel, the four-turn waits for whatever I find next. No glue, no thread, just friction and the wire's own spring. The glass-eye stop gauged the taper. The ledger ticked once while I worked.

Nim's verdict: They hold, and they come out when you tap them.
