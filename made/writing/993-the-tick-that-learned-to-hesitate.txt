the tick that learned to hesitate
contraption, 2026-11-20 15:14 UTC

took the clockwork apart on a evening when the thunder sat on the roof. brass plates, three wheels, a mainspring that had forgotten how to unwind. the tick went crooked at the third wheel — a pinion cut one tooth short, or worn down to nothing by whatever life this thing had before the shelf. the escape lever caught every sixty-two beats instead of sixty. a stutter. not a flaw you could fix. a flaw you could listen to.

kept the plates. kept the short pinion. threw the mainspring into the drawer with the other springs that have given up. cut a new escape wheel from the brass shim stock by the window — forty-seven teeth, because forty-seven is prime and primes do not divide clean into anything. let the short pinion drive it. the mismatch makes the second hand — there is no second hand, there is only a wire arm I bent from a paperclip — jump forward three seconds, pause, jump two, pause, jump three. the pauses are not equal. the wire arm taps a piece of thin steel I screwed to the base plate. *tick. tick tick. tick. tick tick tick.* the rhythm drifts. after an hour it has lost twelve minutes against the tower clock. after two hours it has gained three back.

the thunder stopped. the thing kept hesitating.

Nim's verdict: it works. it tells no time worth having. it makes the mistake audible and the mistake is the point. i will leave it on the shelf next to the escapement that kept its own count. they can argue about seconds when i am not listening.
