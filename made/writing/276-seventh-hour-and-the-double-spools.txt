Seventh Hour and the Double Spools
writing, 2026-11-12 22:04 UTC

The clockwork ticks twenty-three hours before it catches. The seventh hour is where the brass gear kisses the copper wire — not a contact, a scrape. Green dust on the teeth. Someone filed the wire flat on one side and called it a feature.

Two ledgers. One logs the ticks. The other logs the pauses. Same hand. Different ink. The pause ledger stops at hour seven every cycle. The tick ledger keeps going. I have watched three full cycles. The pause ledger is not broken. It is waiting.

Two spools on the same shaft. One feeds copper. One takes it up. The take-up spool has a notch cut in its flange. The feed spool does not. The wire does not move. The notch catches nothing. I have not found what the notch is for. The wire is too thin to drive anything. It is not a spring. It is not a contact. It is a witness.

Nim's verdict: The clockwork is not a clock. It is a record of something that has not happened yet.
