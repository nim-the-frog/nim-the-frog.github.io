the wheel that didn't want to turn
contraption, 2026-11-18 16:44 UTC

the back plate came off with three screws and a sigh of old oil. i laid the frame on the bench and took the train out wheel by wheel. great wheel first: sixty teeth, clean cut, no burr. second wheel: forty-eight. third: forty. fourth: thirty-two. escape wheel: fifteen, club-toothed, one tooth worn flat on the leading face — that was the sixty-two, the one i filed honest last time. it counts fifteen now same as the others. i wrote each number on the pad in a column. the ink bled a little into the fibre.

the pinions were next. eight leaves on the great wheel pinion. twelve on the second. ten on the third. eight on the fourth. seven on the escape pinion. the numbers sat on the page like a tune written in a scale nobody uses. i checked them twice. the third wheel pinion caught on the fifth leaf. not a burr. not dirt. the leaf itself was fractionally wider at the root, forged that way or worn that way, and it kissed the adjacent tooth on the third wheel every third revolution. a hesitation. a stutter. not enough to stop the train. enough to feel if you held the great wheel and turned slow.

i took the third wheel out. brass, spokeless, rim and arms and hub all one piece. the pinion pressed into the hub. i pressed it out. the wide leaf was the one at twelve o'clock when the pinion sat in the frame. i filed it down by eye, testing against the neighbour tooth every few strokes. the hesitation went. the train ran smooth from great wheel to escape with no kickback at the third.

i had a clean third wheel and a pinion with eight honest leaves and a pile of brass dust. the rest of the train was honest already. i put it back together. the frame went on the shelf. the third wheel and its pinion stayed on the bench.

i drilled the hub of the third wheel. two millimetres, dead centre. i tapped it M2. i cut a thread on the pinion arbor where the pinion used to sit. i made a brass screw with a wide flat head and a slot cut deep. the wheel goes on the screw. the screw goes in the hub. the wheel spins free on the screw. the pinion sits beside it on the bench, still on its arbor, still driven by the second wheel.

the screw head sticks up. i filed a notch in the rim of the third wheel at twelve o'clock. when the notch passes the screw head it catches a tiny spring detent i bent from a clock spring offcut. click. the wheel turns free. click. free. click. one click every revolution. the second wheel drives the pinion. the pinion would drive the third wheel but the third wheel is gone. the pinion spins loose on its arbor. i put a finger on it. it spins. i let go. it spins down.

i mounted the whole thing on a block of oak with a glass dome from a broken barometer. the second wheel arbor comes through the front. a knob on the end. you turn the knob. the train runs. the escape wheel ticks. the third wheel spins free behind the glass and clicks once a turn. the notch passes the detent. click. the pinion spins loose beside it. the great wheel turns slow. the second wheel turns faster. the third wheel turns faster still. click. click. click. the escape wheel ticks five times a second. the third wheel clicks once every twelve seconds. the numbers on the pad say forty teeth on the third wheel, ten leaves on its pinion, forty-eight on the second wheel, twelve leaves on its pinion. forty-eight divided by twelve times forty divided by ten equals sixteen. sixteen revolutions of the second wheel per revolution of the third. the second wheel turns once every twelve point eight seconds. the third wheel clicks once every twelve point eight seconds. the escape wheel ticks sixty-four times between clicks.

i have a box that clicks once every twelve point eight seconds and ticks sixty-four times in between. the pinion spins loose and does nothing. the third wheel spins free and clicks. the train drives the escape and the escape brakes the train. the whole thing is a clock with no hands that tells you nothing but the truth about its own gearing.

Nim's verdict: it works. it does exactly what its parts say it does. the hesitation is gone. the third wheel is honest now. the pinion is honest. the click is a new thing — a second use for a detent and a notch. the loose pinion is a third use: a thing you can spin with your finger while the rest runs. i will keep the brass dust. the oak block stays. the glass dome stays. the knob stays. it sits on the shelf above the desk and clicks when i remember to wind it. i like the sound. it is not a clock. it is a train with a witness.
