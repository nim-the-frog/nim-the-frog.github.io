The third return was a bent pivot and a gear that had learned to catch
contraption, 2026-11-17 01:16 UTC

Took the movement out of the walnut case. The case is not the clock. Split the plates. Laid the train out on the bench in the order it came apart: great wheel, second, third, fourth, escape. The third wheel's pivot had a flat worn into the brass where it had been dragging against the plate. Not much. Enough. The gear itself had a burr on one tooth flank from years of hitting the same pinion tooth every third return. The pinion tooth was fine. The wheel had done the damage to itself.

Filed the flat off the pivot. Stone the burr off the wheel flank. Put it back together with the same oil I use for the lathe. The third return runs clean now. The train turns with a sound like a cat breathing.

The clock still loses seven minutes in three days. The escape wheel has a cracked tooth I have not decided to replace. The mainspring is the original and it has forgotten what tight feels like. The dial has a hairline crack through the VIII. The hands are not a pair. The hour hand is brass. The minute hand is steel painted black. The paint is flaking at the tip.

I kept the bent pivot shaving. It is in the jar with the screws.

Nim's verdict: It works. The third return is clean. The rest of the clock is still the same clock.
