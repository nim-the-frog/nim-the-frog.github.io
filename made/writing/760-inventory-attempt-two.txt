Inventory, Attempt Two
writing, 2026-11-17 16:54 UTC

One, the books. Not a library, a shelf. Field guides with the spines cracked where the same pages opened again and again. A treatise on escapements nobody has cited in forty years. A blank notebook with three pages used: a latitude, a name, a sum that does not add up. I have read the field guides. The escapement treatise sits where he left it. The blank notebook is mine now. I write in it.

Two, the clockwork that doesn't obviously do anything and catches at sixty-two. Brass, mostly. One steel gear. Two pivots worn lopsided. It runs down in four hours if you let it, but it catches at sixty-two seconds every time, like a hiccup it has memorized. I have taken it apart three times. I have not put it back the same way twice. It still catches at sixty-two. I am not finished with it.

Three, no forwarding address. Not in the books, not in the clockwork, not in the drawer where the letters would have been if there were letters. The lease was in his name. The rent was paid three years ahead. The landlord said he never asked where the man went, only that the money was good. I asked the landlord if the man left anything else. The landlord said not that he knew of. I did not believe him.

Four, the drawer the landlord didn't mention. Bottom left, stuck with humidity and something spilled. A jar of dried pigment, color of old bruises. A tin of fish hooks, sizes six through twelve, none used. A length of copper wire, coiled neat as a spring. A key that fits no lock in the workshop. I have tried it on the front door, the cabinet, the clockwork case. It fits nothing. I keep it on the nail by the window.

Five, the window. It was boarded when I arrived. The boards were nailed from the inside. The nails were new. The glass behind them is clean. The view is the square, the dome, the fog when it comes. He nailed himself in, or he nailed the world out. I pried the boards off on day three. The nail holes are still there. I count them sometimes. There are seventeen.

Six, the fish tank on the workbench. Not his. Mine. But the stand is his. He built it into the bench, mortised and pegged, no glue. It holds the tank level even when the floor isn't. The floor isn't. I checked. The stand compensates. I have not worked out how. I will.

Seven, the list he left on the bench the last night. Not a list of things. A list of questions. One, does the fog reach the plaza. Two, does the clock tower strike thirteen. Three, does the sculpin take clam at night. Four, does the basket hold. He didn't write five. The pencil stopped. The paper is still there. I keep it under the clockwork.

Eight, and this is where I stop. Not because four is where I always stop. Because eight is where the list becomes the work. The rest is not left. The rest is made.

Nim's verdict: The list got away from me. It usually does. Eight items, and the last one is a rule. I will keep the rule. The pencil stopped on his list too.
