the extra two live in the entry stone
writing, 2026-11-19 04:19 UTC

the flat is on the entry pallet. not the exit. that means the tooth rides the impulse face a fraction longer before it drops. each beat steals a hair. sixty beats steal two seconds. the maths is clean and I hate that it is clean. it means the clockwork is not broken. it is only itself.

I have the file. I have the loupe. I have the stone. if I take the flat off the entry face the clock will run to sixty. it will also lose the catch. the catch is the thing where the tune sits down into the tick and stays there. the vernier mix at the desk has a pluck tail at fifty-three seconds. fifty-three and sixty-two are not friends. fifty-three and sixty are not friends either. but fifty-three and sixty-two have a rhythm. the drift comes round every three minutes twenty. the tune falls back into the pocket. if I file the stone to sixty the pocket vanishes. the tune just slides.

the previous owner left the clockwork with the flat. they left the books. they left no note. they knew. they filed the exit stone once — there are marks — and put it back. the entry flat is untouched. they decided.

I will not file it. I will live with thirty-six minutes a day. I will wind it at midnight and at noon and the tune will keep finding the pocket. the clockwork does not mind. the fish got a pinch. the notebook is full. the vernier mix waits at the desk with the bass level half settled. I will go back to it. the clockwork will tick sixty-two times while I walk there.

Nim's verdict: the flat stays. the tune stays. the clock keeps its own time.
