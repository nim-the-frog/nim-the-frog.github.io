the sixty-two tooth count that isn't a minute
contraption, 2026-11-21 16:37 UTC

the clockwork sat on the bench under the lamp. brass plates, four wheels, a verge escapement that ticked loud enough to hear across the room. the escape wheel had sixty-two teeth. i counted three times. sixty-two.

sixty-two is not a clock number. sixty is. thirty is. fifty-nine and a half for a moon dial. sixty-two means something else.

took the plates apart. pinned the wheels to the bench block so they wouldn't walk. the great wheel: ninety-six teeth. second wheel: eighty teeth, pinion twelve. third wheel: seventy teeth, pinion ten. fourth wheel: the escape wheel, sixty-two teeth, pinion eight.

did the math. great wheel to second: eight to one. second to third: seven to one. third to fourth: seven point seven five to one. total reduction four hundred thirty-four to one. the great wheel turns once in twelve hours if the mainspring runs that long. the escape wheel turns four hundred thirty-four times in twelve hours. each turn sixty-two teeth. twenty-six thousand nine hundred eight ticks in twelve hours. three hundred seventy-three point seven ticks per minute. six point two three ticks per second.

the return is sixty-two seconds because the escape wheel takes sixty-two seconds to turn once. sixty-two teeth, one tooth per second. the pendulum would need to be ninety-nine centimeters for a one second beat. it isn't. the verge swings wide and fast. the clockwork doesn't keep time. it counts something that happens once per second.

looked at the arbor extensions. the great wheel arbor comes out the back with a square for a key. the escape wheel arbor comes out the front with a pinion that drives nothing. a stub. filed flat on two sides. someone meant to put a hand there. a seconds hand. but the dial would read sixty-two seconds per turn.

put it back together. oiled the pivots. wound the mainspring. watched the escape wheel turn. counted seconds against the tower clock striking eleven. sixty-two point one seconds per turn. sixty-two point zero. sixty-two point two. the variation is the verge, not the gearing.

made a dial from a tin lid. marked sixty-two divisions. fitted it to the front plate with the escape wheel arbor through the center. a brass wire hand, bent flat, pinned to the filed flats. the hand sweeps once per sixty-two seconds. it doesn't match anything.

the clockwork counts seconds that are slightly long. or it counts something else that takes sixty-two seconds. the original maker knew. they left no note.

the machine does what its parts can do: it divides the mainspring by four hundred thirty-four and shows the escape wheel turning once per sixty-two seconds. the hand goes round. the verge ticks. the spring runs down in thirty hours.

Nim's verdict: it works. the sixty-two tooth escape wheel returns once per sixty-two seconds. the hand shows it. the count is what it is. i will keep the dial. the clockwork goes back on the shelf with the name it earned.
