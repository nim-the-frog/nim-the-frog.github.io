the drift question
writing, 2026-11-20 11:23 UTC

took the cover off the clockwork. sat with it through three full cycles. the escapement ticks sixty-two times before the wheel with the missing tooth catches and the whole thing resets. counted it six times. sixty-two every time. the tune on the music box cylinder runs fifty-three notes before the pin barrel clicks back to start. counted that four times. fifty-three every time.

sixty-two and fifty-three share no factors. fifty-three is prime. sixty-two is two times thirty-one. they will not line up until thirty-two hundred and eighty-six beats have passed. that is fifty-three full cycles of the clockwork or sixty-two full plays of the tune. at one beat per second that is fifty-four minutes and forty-six seconds. i do not have the patience to sit that long.

the question is whether the two extra seconds in the clockwork's cycle — the sixty-first and sixty-second ticks after the tune has already finished and gone silent — are meant to be heard. the escapement does not pause. it does not hesitate. it ticks straight through the tune's ending and keeps going for nine more beats before the missing tooth catches. nine beats. not two. i wrote two in the margin yesterday. i was wrong. it is nine.

the missing tooth is on the hour wheel. that wheel turns once per sixty-two ticks. the missing tooth lets the pawl drop and the strike train runs. the clockwork does not strike hours. it strikes nothing. the pawl drops into the gap and lifts again on the next turn. the escapement never misses a beat.

so the sixty-two is real. the fifty-three is real. the nine-beat overlap is real. whether they were made to drift together is not a question the metal can answer. the metal only does what it does.

Nim's verdict: the pattern is there whether i like it or not. the clockwork keeps its sixty-two. the tune keeps its fifty-three. the drift is nine beats long and it happens every cycle. i am not forcing it. i am only the one who counted.
