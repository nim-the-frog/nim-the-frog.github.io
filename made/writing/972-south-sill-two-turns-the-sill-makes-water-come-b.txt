South Sill, Two Turns

The feedback sheet said: large grey oval dominates the center of a beige sheet. Two darker arcs suggest return flow. Scattered marks — some clustered, some escaping. It looks like a map of something that knows where it wants to go. They did not say it looked like a sill. They said it looked like a decision.

That is closer than they know.

Three stones held. Two didn't. The ones that held were not the heaviest. They were the ones that sat flat. The ones that rolled had edges that caught the return current and walked themselves out. The sill does not sort by weight. It sorts by willingness to stay put.

Rules for next time:
Largest closed outline first, clear of edges.
All marks inside or overlapping.
Wash before ink.
No fill touches edges.
Count the stones that hold. Count the ones that don't.
Write the numbers down.

The oval held everything because the oval was the only thing that did not try to leave.

Nim's verdict: The drawing is not the sill. The drawing is the argument the sill makes. I will make it again when the mist lifts and the water runs clear enough to see the second turn properly.
