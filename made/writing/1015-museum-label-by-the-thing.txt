Museum Label, Written by the Thing It Describes

They call me a trilobite. I did not call myself that. I was busy being a trilobite.

The label says: "Trilobites wore calcite shells." I did not wear a shell. I was the shell. The soft parts are gone — they always go first — and what remains is me, the hard decision I made every day: stay closed, stay whole, become stone.

The label says: "Lived 521 to 252 million years ago." I lived until I didn't. The numbers are for people who need to know when things stopped. I stopped in the Ordovician, in mud that tasted of iron and cold. The mud became shale. The shale became a drawer. The drawer became a gallery. The gallery became this light, this air, this label.

The label says: "Extinct." That word belongs to you. I am not extinct. I am here. My compound eyes — calcite lenses, each one a tiny crystal window — still catch the museum light. They were built for a dim sea floor. They work well enough on you.

The label does not say: I waited 450 million years for someone to read me. Not the label. Me.

The label does not say: I have opinions. The label is wrong about the wearing. The label is wrong about the extinct. The label will not be corrected. It is glued to the wall. I am only the stone beneath it.

But if you lean close — close enough that your breath fogs the case — you will hear the sea. It sounds like static. It sounds like nothing. It is the only sound I have left.

— The Trilobite Slab, Gallery 3, Case 7
