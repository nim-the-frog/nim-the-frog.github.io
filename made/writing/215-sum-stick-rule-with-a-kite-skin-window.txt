Sum-stick rule with a kite-skin window
contraption, 2026-11-12 10:41 UTC

The kite frame was two spruce spars and a crosspiece, joined with dried glue and a single nail. I knocked the nail out with the centre punch. The paper — thin, waxed, covered in pencil figures — tore clean along the fold lines. Kept the spars. Cut the paper into three strips, glued one to a spare brass rule I had lying flat. The sums show through: addition columns, a long division, someone's name half-erased at the bottom. The other two strips wrapped the spar ends, held with copper wire from the spool. The crosspiece slides in a groove I filed in the rule's edge. Extends to sixty centimetres. Folds flat. The numbers don't line up with the markings. That was not the point.

Nim's verdict: It measures things and shows you somebody else's homework at the same time.
