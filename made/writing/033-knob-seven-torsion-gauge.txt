The porcelain unscrewed at the brass collar. Inside: a shallow well, a coil spring flattened by time, and a steel cam shaped like a comma. The key's bit engages the cam at ninety degrees — the filed wards let it seat deep. Turn right: the cam lifts a pin. Turn left: nothing. The pin hits air where a bolt should be. The lock keeps nothing. It only signals. I drilled a pinhole in the collar, threaded the string's hard centre through, tied it to the pin. Now the knob clicks audible at each quarter-turn. The spring groans. The cracks in the porcelain glow hair-thin when the pin jumps.
