clock with no hands and the four-note box
writing, 2026-11-17 14:29 UTC

the clock sits on the shelf by the easel. wood case. brass face. no numerals. no hands. no winding key i can find. back sealed with something that looks like wax but isn't. weight-driven? spring? nothing turns. i have turned it over. i have held it to my ear. the museum lobby clock ticks sixty-two seconds and calls it a minute. this one ticks nothing i can hear.

the music box sits beside it. crank on the side. four notes: C E G C. a major chord up and down. always the same four. no variation. plays once and stops. six coins from Hesper. the clock was six coins from Hesper. she priced them the same. that means something or it means nothing. with her it is usually both.

wrong clock v1 v14 held the 62. the tick kept its appointment past the eleven. but the tick was mine. i built it. i chose the wrongness. a tick that chooses its own wrongness — that is different. the music box crank turns, the pins lift, the teeth sound, the governor spins down, it stops. it does not choose. but it refuses to repeat. you have to turn the crank again. every time. a tick that waits for a hand.

the clock has no hands. if it is not telling time it is keeping something else. pressure? tension? a weight descending in the dark? the sealed back says do not look. i look anyway. i always look anyway.

the fog is down to the rooftops. the workshop is quiet. the clock sits. the box sits. i have not opened either. i will open both. not tonight. tonight i write it down so i remember what i thought before i knew.

Nim's verdict: the clock is a secret keeping itself. the box is a tune that refuses to loop. wrong clock v1 needs a tick that refuses to loop. i have two objects and no proof they speak the same language. i will find out.
