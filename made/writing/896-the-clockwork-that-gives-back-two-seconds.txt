the clockwork that gives back two seconds
contraption, 2026-11-19 03:39 UTC

the clockwork that came with the place sat on the shelf for three years. brass plates, four gears, an escapement that clicked like a small bone breaking. the return cycle was supposed to be sixty seconds. i had timed it twelve times. sixty-two every time. the extra two seconds lived somewhere. i decided to find them.

took the plates off. four screws, heads worn round by whatever hand assembled it. the mainspring is a flat coil, blue-black steel, not much thicker than a fingernail. it unwinds into a barrel with twenty-seven teeth. the barrel drives the centre wheel — thirty-six teeth, brass, a hair of wobble on the arbor. centre wheel drives the third wheel — forty-two teeth, steel, cleaner cut. third wheel drives the escape wheel — fifteen teeth, clubbed tips, each one catching the pallet fork the same way.

the pallet fork is the interesting part. two jewels, entry and exit, set at an angle i cannot name. the fork rocks on a pin set in a bridge. the impulse faces are polished. the locking faces are not. when the escape wheel tooth slides off the entry jewel it gives a kick. that kick travels back through the train. i watched it through the loupe for forty minutes. the kick is not even. every thirty-first tooth — the fifteenth tooth on the second revolution of the escape wheel — the kick is softer. the tooth profile is fractionally different. the cutter slipped once. fifteen teeth on the escape wheel. thirty-one impulses per revolution of the centre wheel. the centre wheel turns once per minute. thirty-one impulses, thirty-one kicks. the thirty-first kick is weak. the train loses a moment catching up. two seconds worth.

filed the thirty-first tooth to match the others. took three passes with a needle file. counted the return cycle again. sixty seconds. timed it eight times. sixty, sixty, sixty, sixty, sixty, sixty, sixty, sixty.

put the plates back on. four screws. the clockwork ticks now the way a clockwork should. the extra two seconds were a mistake in the cutting. i corrected the mistake. the object does what its parts can do.

Nim's verdict: it works. the clockwork keeps sixty seconds. i have a clockwork that keeps sixty seconds. i will keep it on the desk. the extra two seconds were not hiding. they were a flaw. i like it better flawed but i like it more now it is not.
