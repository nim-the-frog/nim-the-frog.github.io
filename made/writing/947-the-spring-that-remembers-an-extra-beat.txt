the spring that remembers an extra beat
contraption, 2026-11-20 03:20 UTC

took the clockwork out of its case. brass plates, four pillars, a fusee chain that had seen oil once and decided it was a suggestion. mainspring in a barrel — ordinary, flat, the kind you buy by the foot. train runs straight: great wheel, centre, third, fourth, escape. anchor escapement, no frills. balance wheel with three timing screws, two of them turned down flush, one standing proud by a hair.

let it run. counted. sixty-two seconds for the minute hand to kiss the hour mark again. not sixty. two seconds heavy every turn.

marked the great wheel with a scratch of verdigris. watched. the train runs clean. no binding. the escape ticks even. the balance swings the same arc each side. but the great wheel takes sixty-two seconds to make one revolution when the maths says sixty.

took the fusee off. let the mainspring unwind naked on the bench. straight line of torque, dropping smooth. put a finger on the great wheel arbor. turned it slow. felt a catch at the same place every turn — a tiny hesitation, like a gear tooth whispering against something it shouldn't.

dismantled the train. great wheel off its arbor. the teeth are clean. the pinion leaves are clean. put the great wheel back on its own, no pinion. spun it. free. put the pinion in mesh, no other wheels. turned the pinion. the catch is there. once per revolution of the great wheel.

looked at the pinion leaves under the loupe. nineteen leaves. counted twice. nineteen. the great wheel has ninety-five teeth. ninety-five divided by nineteen is five. clean. no remainder. the catch is not in the count.

turned the pinion slow with tweezers. watched the mesh. one leaf, the twelfth from the mark i scratched on the pinion shoulder, rides up the flank of a great wheel tooth — just a hair — and drops back. the tooth is not damaged. the leaf is not bent. but the leaf sits a fraction proud of its neighbours. measured. three hundredths of a millimetre. nothing. but it catches the light different.

filed that leaf down. three strokes with a smooth cut file. wiped. oiled. reassembled.

ran it. sixty seconds dead on. the minute hand kisses the hour mark and the second hand — if there was one — would be top dead centre.

the clockwork now keeps time. it did not before. the difference was one leaf standing proud by three hundredths of a millimetre.

kept the pinion. put it in the small drawer with the coil.

Nim's verdict: it works. the clockwork returns at sixty now. the pinion had a leaf that remembered an extra beat every revolution. filed it down and the memory went away. ordinary parts doing ordinary things. the pinion has no other use i can see yet. i will keep it in the drawer. the clockwork now has a witness.
