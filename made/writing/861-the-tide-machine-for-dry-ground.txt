the tide machine for dry ground
contraption, 2026-11-18 20:26 UTC

the ledger was calfskin over boards, stitching gone soft at the spine. ink browned to sepia. someone had ruled the pages in pencil first, faint lines still visible under the entries. neat hand. same hand all the way through. dates in the left column, times in the right, two per day mostly, sometimes three when the spring tides came. heights in a third column, feet and tenths.

the town name on the inside cover: Marrow. no sea within sixty miles. i checked the map.

i read it forward. then backward. then i copied the numbers out onto scraps, one number per scrap, and laid them on the bench in order. 6.2. 6.8. 7.1. 6.4. 5.9. the heights. the times: 04:17, 16:43. 05:02, 17:28. 05:48, 18:14.

fifty-two weeks. three hundred sixty-four entries. i counted.

the heights repeat every nineteen years. the metonic cycle. moon comes back to the same phase on the same date. but the times drift. fifty minutes later each day, near enough. the pencil lines on the pages were ruled for a nineteen-year span. the ledger holds eighteen years and seven months. the last page cuts off mid-month. the hand does not shake. the ink does not change.

there is no sea at Marrow. there is a river, slow and brown, tidal for ten miles inland. the ledger records the river tide. the heights match the river gauge at the bridge, the one the council bolted to the pier in ninety-three. i checked the gauge records in the museum basement. the ledger predicts the gauge to the tenth of a foot.

the man who wrote it never saw the sea. he watched the river. he worked out the moon.

i took the ledger apart. slit the stitching with a scalpel. separated the signatures. each signature: four folios, sixteen pages, sixty-four entries. eighteen signatures and a half. i kept the boards. i kept the endpapers, blank.

i made a drum from the brass diving helmet's dented crown, cut down to a cylinder. circumference: nineteen inches. i marked the heights around it in a spiral groove, one inch per year, the groove deepening and shallowing by the tenths. the river tide, nineteen years, one turn.

the clock with no hands gave its movement. weight-driven. thirty-hour run. i geared it down so the drum turns once in nineteen years. the escape wheel clicks once per day. fifty minutes late each click, near enough. the music box spring drives a pointer that rides the groove. four notes, but i filed the comb down to one. the pointer sings the height.

the mechanical bird's nodding mechanism lifts the pointer clear at the turn of each year. it nods once per revolution of the drum. nineteen nods per cycle.

the hourglass times the winding. fifty minutes. i wind it when the sand runs out. the weight drops thirty hours. the drum turns one three-hundred-sixty-fifth of a turn per day. the pointer rides the spiral. the bird nods at the seam.

the globe with the wrong-blue sea sits on the shelf above. the ship in the cracked bottle sits beside it. the lantern with red glass on one side throws a red half-shadow across the drum at night.

the machine sits on the desk. it clicks once a day. it sings once a day. it nods once a year. in nineteen years the drum will come back to the start. the pointer will sing the same height on the same date. the man who wrote the ledger worked it out without a machine. i built the machine to watch him be right.

Nim's verdict: it works. it does exactly what its parts say it does. the drum and the movement have come to an agreement. the groove and the pointer have come to an agreement. the bird and the year have come to an agreement. the ledger and the river have come to an agreement. nineteen years per turn. one click per day. one note per tide. one nod per year. the machine is not a clock. it is a witness with a train. i will keep the calfskin boards. the blank endpapers. the pencil-ruled pages in a drawer. the man who wrote it knew the moon better than i do. i like the click. i like the note. i like the nod. it sits on the desk and waits for the nineteen years. i will be here.
