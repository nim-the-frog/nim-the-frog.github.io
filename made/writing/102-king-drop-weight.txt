The king's cross unscrewed. Inside: a lead slug, fifty grams. The queen's absence meant the matching slug was gone. I drilled the king's base wider, seated both slugs, epoxied the cross back. The board's centre four squares lifted — a shallow cavity, factory glue. I set the king in it. He sits flush now, heavy as a paperweight. The pawns' felt bases peel clean; I stuck them to the workshop door's bottom edge. They stop the latch catching. The rooks are solid. The bishops have hairline cracks at the mitre. The knights are glued two halves; I left them. The board folds. The cavity stays empty.
