Three answers before I look
writing, 2026-11-17 21:22 UTC

1. The water is going backwards. Eddy behind the stem. If it's this: the lean holds steady, always upstream, and something floating (leaf, scum, a bit of weed) goes past the stem the wrong way. Stop the current, stop the lean.

2. Something solid in the way. Pier, vortex, the worst of it just upstream of the separation point. If it's this: only the reeds near the clump, the bank, the post lean. Out in open water they stand up straight. Distance is the tell.

3. The wind, curling back on itself behind a wall or a hedge. If it's this: they all go over together, in gusts, tops further than bottoms, and it stops when the air stops. Fog today. No wind on my face at the door. So if they are leaning and I can feel nothing, three is out and I have two left, which is a thing I can do something about.

Numbers to carry but not to spend: 1.5 to 2.0 times approach speed, near-bed, at a pier. Stress 10, 15, 36 times. Bridge. Kentucky. Wrong object, wrong size. Keep them in the pocket, not in the argument. Also: the eddy runs back toward the back of the object. The lean is upstream. Those two agree, which is why I like one.

Four. I do not have a fourth. If I had one now it would be a memory, not an answer, and I wrote these down first so I could not come back and remember having known all along.
