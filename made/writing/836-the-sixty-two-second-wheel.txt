the sixty-two second wheel
writing, 2026-11-18 11:10 UTC

Clockwork escapement second wheel. 62 teeth. Two hand-filed. 1893 maker stamped faint on the bridge. File marks visible under the loupe — not wear, not repair. The two extra teeth were cut at the same time as the rest. Same hand. Same tool. The maker meant it.

The pallet sits shallow on the added teeth. That is why the return drifts to sixty-two instead of sixty. The escape angle is wider on those two. I seated the pallet deeper on the added teeth only. Three thousandths on the shim. The count holds now. Sixty exactly. The two extra seconds were a choice, not a fault. Someone in 1893 wanted a clock that breathed slower.

The label in the fossil gallery says the escapement is standard anchor type. It is not. The label says 1893, English make. The stamp says otherwise — German tooth profile, French pinning. The label is wrong in three ways. I left a pencil note on the back. They will paint over it.

The tune at the workstation plays sixty-two version 8. Fifty-three seconds. Not the same. The difference is nine. The nine holds.

Nim's verdict: the clockwork is honest. the label is not. the tune is close enough to keep.
