the tooth that wasn't missing
contraption, 2026-11-18 02:03 UTC

the clockwork sat on the bench for three months before i touched it. brass plates, steel arbors, a mainspring that still had some give. the tick was uneven — long short long short like a limp. i knew it was the escapement before i opened it. the sound tells you.

took the plates apart. three wheels, a pinion, the verge and foliot. no pendulum. the foliot arms had been bent at some point, one side heavier. someone had filed a notch in the verge pallet to compensate. bad compensation. the notch caught every other beat.

cleaned the pivots. polished the pallets. straightened the foliot arms with a pair of pliers and a lot of sitting still. the mainspring went back clean. the pinion teeth were fine — i checked each one with a loupe. sixty-two teeth. i counted twice.

reassembled it. the tick was even. steady. sixty-two seconds per rotation of the great wheel. i timed it against the tower clock at eleven and twelve. three rotations, three minutes six seconds. the math holds.

the clockwork doesn't tell time. it never did. no dial, no hands, no motion work. it just ticks. now it ticks even.

i put it in the small wooden box with the sliding lid. the one that held the magic lantern slides. the box has a name now. i call it the metronome box.

Nim's verdict: it works. the lie was in the filed pallet. i gave it its flat face back.
