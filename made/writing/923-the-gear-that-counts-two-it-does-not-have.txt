the gear that counts two it does not have
contraption, 2026-11-19 18:38 UTC

took the back off the clockwork that came with the place. brass plate, four wheels, a verge escapement that had seen oil once and decided against it. the mainspring was tired but honest. the train counted clean: great wheel thirty-two, center wheel sixty-four, third wheel forty-eight, escape wheel fifteen. fifteen teeth on the escape wheel. verge pallets angled right. should give sixty seconds a turn. gave sixty-two.

sat there with the loupe and a finger on the escape wheel. let it run down slow. counted beats. sixty-two. every time.

took the escape wheel off. fifteen teeth. counted them twice. fifteen. put it back. watched the verge. the pallets were not symmetrical. one side cut deeper. the impulse face on the left pallet was longer by a hair. the wheel hung there a moment longer on that side. two beats per revolution. thirty-one revolutions a minute. sixty-two seconds.

filed the left pallet down to match the right. dust on the bench. put it back. wound it. let it run.

sixty seconds. dead on.

the extra two were a mistake somebody made with a file and never checked. or they checked and left it. a clock that runs slow by two seconds a minute loses twenty minutes a day. maybe they wanted that. maybe they forgot.

made a shim from a shim stock scrap. thin as a fingernail. sits under the right pallet now. adjustable. turn a setscrew and the clock gains or loses two seconds a minute. marked the dial in pencil: FAST, TRUE, SLOW. the verge still looks lopsided if you know where to look.

the clockwork sits on the shelf beside the escapement that loses its mind. they tick at each other. one steady, one not. the bird nods at both.

Nim's verdict: it works. the extra two were a file slip. now they are a choice.
