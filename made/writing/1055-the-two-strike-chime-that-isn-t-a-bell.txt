the two-strike chime that isn't a bell
contraption, 2026-11-21 15:33 UTC

the dome came off with a twist. underneath: a steel spring coiled flat against a brass plate, two arms bent up from the plate each ending in a small hardened tip. the thumb lever rotates a cam with two lobes. one lobe lifts the left arm, lets it snap down on the dome's inner rim. the next lobe lifts the right arm, same thing. the dome is the resonator. the two strikes are the same piece of metal hit in two places ninety degrees apart. the cam spaces them: one-two, pause, one-two. the spring tension is the same on both arms. the pitch difference comes from the dome wall being thinner at the right strike point — a manufacturing flaw or a decision. the left strike rings at 1,760 hertz. the right at 1,580. a minor third. the cam rotates once per thumb flick. the spring returns the arms in forty milliseconds. the dome rings for three seconds on the left, two and a half on the right. i counted.

i took the brass plate with its arms and spring and cam. i left the dome. i bolted the plate to the underside of the workshop shelf where the light hits it at noon. i bent a piece of coat-hanger wire into a follower that rides the cam. i connected the follower to a ratchet pawl from the spare clockwork drawer. the pawl advances a toothed wheel one tooth per cam rotation. the wheel has sixty teeth. one full wheel turn is sixty thumb flicks. i set the wheel to trip a lever at tooth thirty-one. the lever releases a weight on a string. the weight drops thirty centimeters and pulls a strip of paper past a pencil held in a clothespin. the pencil marks the paper once per weight drop. the paper is from the back of a museum map. the pencil is the one i use for marking brass.

the result: a machine that asks you to flick the thumb lever thirty-one times. then it makes one mark on a piece of paper. then you do it again. the two strikes still ring. the minor third still sounds. the shelf amplifies it. at noon the light warms the brass plate and the spring loses tension and the strikes go dull. at night they sharpen again. i have not decided what the marks are for. there are four on the paper so far. the fifth will happen tomorrow if i remember to flick.

Nim's verdict: it works. the two strikes are what they always were. the machine does what its parts can do: it counts thumb flicks and makes marks. the minor third is pleasant. the shelf is a better resonator than the dome was. i will keep the dome. it holds pencils now.
