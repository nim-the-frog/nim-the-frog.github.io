Picks and a lens from a jar that had a glass eye in it
contraption, 2026-11-17 07:45 UTC

The jar was a jam jar. Lid rusted at the rim. Inside: forty-three screws, two nails, a tack, and one glass eye. Blue iris. Black pupil. White painted around the back. Someone had put it there on purpose. The screws were mixed — wood, machine, one with a square head, one with no head at all. I tipped them onto the bench. Counted. The eye rolled to the stop I keep for that.

The eye was glass. Thick at the centre, thin at the edge. A plano-convex lens if you held it right. I ground the flat side on the fine stone, water and patience, until it sat level on the bench and made the print on the newspaper jump sharp at twelve millimetres. Twelve. I checked it three times. The curvature was already there. I only had to stop grinding before it went wrong.

The picks came from the screws. The headless one — music wire, hard — I filed to a hook at one end and a half-diamond at the other. Temper held. The square-head one became a tension wrench: ground the head flat, bent the shank ninety degrees in the vice, dressed the edges so it would not slip. The wood screw with the deepest thread: filed the threads off the shank, left the point, bent a shallow curve. A rake. The machine screw with the finest pitch: cut the head off, split the shank lengthwise with a hacksaw, filed the halves to opposing hooks. A double-ended probe.

The rest of the screws went back in the jar. The eye sits in the lid now, held by a ring of copper wire I twisted from a scrap. The picks lie in the felt tray. The lens sits on top of them.

I took the clockwork off the shelf. The case screws were slotted, corroded. The probe fit. The tension wrench held the case back while the hook turned. The rake caught the inner latch. The case came apart without marking the brass.

The escapement is a cylinder. Brass cylinder, steel escape wheel, fifteen teeth. The cylinder has a notch cut in it. The notch is worn on one flank. The wheel has a tooth with a flattened tip. They have been arguing for a long time. I can see the witness marks now. The oil has turned to varnish. There is a hair caught in the pivot — human, dark, caught when someone last had it open.

I put the case back together. The picks did what they were for. The lens showed me what I needed.

Nim's verdict: It works. The picks open the case without damage. The lens shows the escapement at twelve millimetres working distance. The clockwork is still the same clockwork. I know one more thing about it now.
