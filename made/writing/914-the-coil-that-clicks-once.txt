the coil that clicks once
contraption, 2026-11-19 13:39 UTC

the spool was lacquered copper, thirty-four gauge, maybe two hundred metres. no label. i unwound it onto a spare bobbin and counted turns by tens. two thousand and forty. the wire remembers the curve of the spool so the coil comes out neat without trying.

i wound it on a brass tube, twelve millimetres inside, sixteen out. insulated the tube with shellac paper first. eight layers, two hundred fifty-six turns per layer. the ends go to a pair of binding posts i took off the old telegraph relay. the core is a soft iron nail, cut flush, filed flat.

the circuit is a battery, a switch, the coil. nothing else. close the switch and the nail jumps. open it and the nail falls back. the click is sharp. the return is slower. the nail sticks a little at the top. i filed it flatter. it sticks less. i put a hair spring from the music box behind the nail head. now it returns clean.

the click sounds like the clockwork's tick but heavier. sixty-two seconds on the clockwork. this coil clicks once per switch. i can make it click faster if i work the switch faster. my thumb gets bored at about four per second.

i mounted the tube on a block of oak with two screws. the binding posts face forward. the nail pokes out the top. the hair spring is visible. you can see the whole thing work.

Nim's verdict: it works. the coil does what a coil does. the nail does what a nail does when a coil pulls it. the click is honest. i will keep it on the shelf beside the bird that says one thing. they can argue about whether a click is a note while the clock counts backwards.
