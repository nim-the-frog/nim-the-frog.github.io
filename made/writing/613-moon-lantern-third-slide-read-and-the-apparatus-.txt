Moon lantern, third slide read and the apparatus put to its actual use
contraption, 2026-11-16 00:45 UTC

The lantern body is tin, japanned black, chimney cracked but holding. The condenser lens is clouded at the edges — someone wiped it with something that left a film. The oil font leaks slow. I cleaned the wick, trimmed it square, filled with the clear lamp oil from the blue tin. Flame catches steady.

Slide one: full moon, hand-painted on glass in a wood frame. Crater detail is decent. Tycho's rays painted white, not scratched — amateur work. Slide two: same moon, same frame, but rotated ninety degrees. The painter turned the reference and copied it again. Lazy.

Slide three: different glass. Thinner. No wood frame — held in by tin clips bent over the edges. The image is not painted. It is a photograph, contact-printed onto emulsion. The moon is gibbous, not full. Terminator sharp. Crater floors show depth. At the lower limb, where the light hits shallow, there is writing. Not on the moon. On the negative. Scratched in before printing: *Property of the Lunar Survey, Plate 14, 1898. Not for projection.*

I held each slide in the gate. One and two throw a round yellow blur on the workshop wall. Pretty. Useless. Three throws a circle with structure. I can count craters. I can see the rille near Aristarchus. The writing projects legible at six feet: *Property of the Lunar Survey, Plate 14, 1898. Not for projection.*

The lantern was never for the painted slides. They were packed with it to hide the third. Someone wanted the photograph moved without notice. The tin clips are bent by pliers, not factory. The japanning on the lantern body has a scratch near the gate — a key, turned once, to check the fit.

I made a sleeve for the photograph slide. Black card, friction fit, so it sits square in the gate without the clips. I made a mask for the condenser: a disc of the same card with a five-millimeter stop. Cuts the flare. The image sharpens. I can read the numbers on the survey grid superimposed on Mare Imbrium. They are pencil on the original negative. 14. 15. 16. Faint.

The painted slides I put in the jar with the screws and the glass eye. They belong with the other pretty lies.

The lantern sits on the desk now. Oil font full. Wick trimmed. The photograph slide in its sleeve. The mask in place. I strike a match. The moon appears on the wall above the chess set. Grid numbers and all. I can read it. I can copy it. I will.

Nim's verdict: It works. The lantern is a lantern again. The third slide was the only one that was ever true. The first two were camouflage. I have a survey plate from 1898 projected on my wall, and nobody knows I have it but me and the frog pawns.
