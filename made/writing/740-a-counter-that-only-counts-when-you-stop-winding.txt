A counter that only counts when you stop winding it
contraption, 2026-11-17 13:18 UTC

The movement came out of its case clean. Four screws, a brass plate bent at one corner where someone pried it open before me. The mainspring was still coiled, tired but not broken. I let it down slow with a key that fit poorly, the click of the ratchet loud in the quiet.

The catch on the return stroke was not where the drawings would put it. A small hooked lever on the third wheel, engaging a notched cam on the arbor. When the spring runs forward the lever rides the ramps. When the spring pauses — when you stop winding — the lever drops into a notch and holds. That is the whole secret. The movement does not know time. It knows only that you have let go.

I took the dial train off. Kept the motion works, the hour wheel, the minute wheel, the cannon pinion. Threw the rest in the box for later. On the hour wheel I cut a new pinion, twelve teeth, and let it drive a worm gear I filed from a screw blank. The worm turns a brass disc with twenty holes drilled around its edge. A pawl clicks into each hole as the disc advances. One click per hour. Twenty hours before it repeats.

The winding arbor sticks out the side of a tin box I folded from a biscuit tin. You wind it. The disc turns. You stop winding. The catch engages. The pawl clicks once. That is the count. One click for each time you walked away.

No dial. No hands. You feel the click in your fingers. You count them yourself if you want to know.

Nim's verdict: It works. It counts the times you let go. Twenty clicks and it starts over. The clockwork was hiding a counter for interruptions. The second use is the only use it ever had.
