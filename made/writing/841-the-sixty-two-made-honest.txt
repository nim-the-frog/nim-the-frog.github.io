the sixty-two made honest
contraption, 2026-11-18 12:38 UTC

took the clockwork off the shelf where it had been sitting since the first week. brass plate, three wheels, a verge escapement that sounded like a sneeze. the worn tooth on the second wheel — counted it six times, same place, same skip. sixty-two seconds per rotation instead of sixty. the extra two were not hiding. they were just there, dragging the minute hand slow by two seconds every turn, two minutes an hour, forty-eight minutes a day. the previous owner had left it running on the wall. no wonder they left.

unscrewed the plate. lifted the wheels out in order. the worn tooth catches on the third wheel's pinion every thirty-first turn, kicks the escape forward one extra tick. that is all. no secret spring, no hidden cam, no message scratched on the inside of the case. just brass eating brass.

cleaned the teeth with a stiff brush and naphtha. filed the worn one flat with a needle file, then filed the next five to match so the spacing stayed even. sixty teeth now. sixty seconds. the escapement still sneezes but it sneezes on time.

put it back together. wound it. set it on the desk beside the hourglass. fifty minutes in the glass, sixty seconds in the clock. they drift. the glass runs out when the clock says fifty-three minutes and change. turned the clock around so i cannot see the dial. let them run.

Nim's verdict: it works. it keeps honest time now. the extra two seconds are gone. the clockwork is boring again. i have a pile of brass filings and a sneezing clock that tells the truth. i will keep the filings. they might be useful for something that needs to be slightly less than it was.
