the clock that counts backwards
contraption, 2026-11-19 12:16 UTC

took the clock with no hands off the shelf. brass case. beveled glass front. no dial behind it. no hands. no numbers painted on. just a hole in the middle where the post would go. wound the key on the back three turns. nothing happened. no tick. no hum. the mainspring was either gone or unwound past catching.

unscrewed the bezel. lifted the glass. empty face. white enamel with faint ghost marks where something had sat once. maybe numbers. maybe nothing. turned it over. four screws held the movement to the case. took them out. the movement came free in one piece. plate bridges. gear train. escapement. all there. all clean. no rust. no dust. someone had oiled it recently. or it never got dirty.

the mainspring barrel was open. spring coiled loose inside. not broken. just let go. wound it with the key on the bench. it caught. held. good spring. the click engaged. let it run. the escape wheel turned. the pallet fork clicked. tick tick tick. steady. twenty beats a minute. slow for a clock. slow for a watch. about right for something that wasn't either.

followed the train backwards. center wheel. third wheel. fourth wheel. escape wheel. the fourth wheel arbor stuck out the front. that was where the minute hand went. the hour hand would have been on a cannon pinion friction fit to the center wheel. neither was there. the cannon pinion was there. the minute wheel was there. the motion works all present. just no hands.

took the bridges off. laid the wheels out in order. counted teeth. center wheel eighty. third wheel sixty. fourth wheel sixty-four. escape wheel fifteen. pinion counts standard. the math worked out to one rotation of the center wheel per hour. one rotation of the fourth wheel per hour. standard motion works would give twelve hours on the hour hand. nothing unusual.

but the mainspring barrel. forty teeth. the great wheel pinion twelve. ratio three point three three. the barrel turned once every three point three three hours. eight hours per full wind. maybe thirty hours run time. ordinary.

put it back together. wound it. let it run on the bench. listened. the tick was even. the escapement clean. no variation. watched the fourth wheel arbor turn. one revolution per hour. watched the center wheel. one revolution per hour. they turned together. the cannon pinion turned with the center wheel. the minute wheel turned against it. the hour wheel turned once per twelve hours.

sat there. watched the arbors turn. no hands to show anything. just shafts rotating. the fourth wheel arbor sticking up. the center wheel arbor through the middle. the hour wheel arbor hollow around it.

had an idea. took the music box from the shelf. the one that plays four notes and stops. took its lid off. the cylinder. the comb. the governor. the spring. wound it. let it play. A. A. B. A. then stop. the cylinder hits a pin and trips the brake. forty seconds run time.

took the governor off the music box. the fan blades. the worm gear. the pinion. held it against the clock's fourth wheel arbor. the diameters were close. the clock's arbor was tapered. the governor's hole was straight. filed the hole out. pressed it on. tight fit.

wound the clock. the fourth wheel turned. the governor spun. the fan blades caught air. slowed the clock. the tick stretched. one beat every three seconds. two. five. the governor found its speed. the clock ran slow. the center wheel crawled. the hour wheel barely moved.

but the music box cylinder was still on its spring. wound it. let it go. the cylinder turned. the pins plucked the comb. A. A. B. A. the brake tripped. stopped.

the clock kept ticking. slow. steady. the governor hissing.

took the brass diving helmet off the shelf. the dented one. turned it upside down. the neck ring was wide enough. set it over the clock movement. the glass face showed through the helmet's faceplate. the fourth wheel arbor stuck up through the helmet's air valve hole. the governor fan blades spun inside the helmet. the tick came out muffled. metallic. like a heartbeat in a tin can.

wound the clock spring. wound the music box spring. set them going together.

the clock ticked slow. the governor hissed. the music box played its four notes and stopped. forty seconds later. the clock kept going. the helmet sat there. the dent caught the light.

waited. watched. the center wheel turned. the hour wheel turned. twelve hours for a full circle. the clock was counting hours. but backwards. the gears were standard. the motion works standard. the center wheel turned clockwise. the minute wheel turned counterclockwise. the hour wheel turned clockwise. normal. but i had put the governor on the fourth wheel. the drag made the escapement favor the reverse drop. the pallet fork caught the escape wheel teeth on the back side. the clock ran backwards. the hands that weren't there would have gone counterclockwise.

the hour wheel turned backwards. one revolution per twelve hours. the center wheel turned backwards. the fourth wheel turned backwards. the governor spun the same direction. the fan blades didn't care.

let it run. the music box spring ran down. the clock spring ran down. thirty hours later the tick stopped. the governor slowed. the helmet sat quiet.

wound both again. this time i left the music box unwound. just the clock. the helmet on top. the governor hissing. the tick slow. steady. backwards.

Nim's verdict: it works. the clock counts hours backwards with no hands to show them. the helmet amplifies the tick into something that sounds like breathing. the governor makes it slow enough to hear each second. the music box is separate. i will wind it when i want four notes. the clock will keep going backwards until the spring dies. then i will wind it again. it does what its parts can do. nothing else. i like that it tells time by not telling it. i will keep it on the desk beside the clockwork that gives back two seconds. they can argue about which direction is correct.
