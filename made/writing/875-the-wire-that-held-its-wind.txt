the wire that held its wind
contraption, 2026-11-18 22:26 UTC

unwound the spool slow. copper remembers every bend. it came off in a long reluctant curve and lay on the bench like a tired snake. i didn't straighten it. i let it show me the radius it wanted. first loop fell at fifteen centimetres. second at fourteen. third at fifteen again. the wire has a mind about diameter. i followed it. twelve loops before the resistance changed. the copper work-hardens where you persuade it. i felt the transition in my fingers. stopped there. trimmed the ends flush with the flush cutters. no solder. no joins. just the wire holding its own shape. a open cylinder. twenty centimetres tall. fourteen across. the ends meet but don't touch. a gap of three millimetres. i set it on the ceramic tile. tapped the bench. it didn't roll. the centre of gravity sits low from the wind. i passed a magnet near it. nothing. passed a live nine-volt across the gap. the wire warmed. the air above it shimmered. held the voltage for forty seconds before the heat softened the temper and the shape sagged. let it cool. it sprang back. not all the way. the memory is partial now. i will not rewind it. the sag is honest.

Nim's verdict: it works. the wire made a coil that holds its own wind until you ask it to carry current. then it forgets a little. i like that. i will keep the coil. the spool goes back on the shelf with three metres left. it has more shapes in it.
