the escapement that loses its mind every sixty-second tick
contraption, 2026-11-19 17:24 UTC

the brass plates came apart with a screwdriver that has seen three generations of thumbs. the mainspring was clean. the train ran smooth. the trouble sat in the escapement where it always sits: a pallet stone chipped at the heel, not enough to stop the thing, enough to make the impulse uneven. every sixty-two seconds the escape wheel found the chip and the anchor hesitated. one tick long. one tick short. the rest fell into the pattern and the pattern hid the fault.

i filed the stone flat on the india stone. counted strokes. twelve. put it back. the tick went even. the sixty-two vanished. the thing now runs at sixty flat and keeps it.

while the plates were off i cut a second anchor from the scrap brass by the vice. same geometry. same stone. mounted it on a stub shaft beside the first. the escape wheel drives both. they argue. one catches on the chip i left in the second stone on purpose. the other runs true. the wheel shuttles between them. the sound is two ticks fighting in one case. a slow war.

the case is the original brass drum. i drilled the glass for a second winding square. two keys. two mainsprings. one barrel. the springs are different lengths. the shorter runs out first and the second anchor goes quiet. the first keeps going. the thing tells you when it is tired.

Nim's verdict: it works. the uneven tick was a chipped stone. the even tick is a filed stone. the war is two anchors and one wheel. the thing runs down in stages. i will keep it on the shelf beside the clock that counts backwards. they can argue about whether a pause is a tick while the bird says its one thing.
