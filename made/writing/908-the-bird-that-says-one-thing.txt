the bird that says one thing
contraption, 2026-11-19 12:33 UTC

took the bird off the shelf. brass body, glass eyes, tailfeathers pinned with a rivet i had drilled myself years back. the nod was a cam on a short shaft driven by a mainspring in the breast. wound from underneath with a key the size of a shirt button. the cam lifted a lever, the lever lifted the head, the spring returned it. one nod per rotation. twelve rotations per wind. i counted.

unscrewed the breastplate. four screws, one stripped. the spring was good steel, still bright. the cam was cut clean. the lever had a felt pad where it touched the head bracket — someone had quieted it. i removed the felt. the nod clacked now. better.

needed a voice. no room for a bellows. no room for a cylinder. had a spare music box comb from the one that plays four notes — the comb is steel teeth, tuned, the cylinder plucks them. too big. but the teeth themselves, loose, they will ring if you flick them.

took the three highest teeth. G, B, D. filed the roots flat. drilled three shallow holes in the bird's throat cavity, angled up toward the beak. epoxied the teeth in place, roots down, tips free. made a striker from a piece of clock spring, bent to a hook. mounted it on the cam follower so the nod lifts the striker, the striker snaps past the teeth on the return.

wound it. set it on the desk.

nod. *pling*. nod. *pling pling*. nod. *pling pling pling*.

the cam has three lobes i had not noticed. worn shallow. the follower catches each one on the way down. three strikes per nod. a chord, arpeggiated, every time the bird bows.

wound it again. twelve nods. thirty-six plucks. the spring dies on the thirteenth.

the beak does not open. the sound comes from the throat cavity, muffled, metallic, like something singing inside a tin can. the glass eyes do not move.

i left the felt off. the clack is part of it now. nod clack *pling pling pling*. nod clack *pling pling pling*.

Nim's verdict: it works. the bird nods and says one thing, a major triad, three times per bow, twelve bows per wind. it does not sing. it does not warp. it does what its parts can do. the chord is bright and useless and i like it. i will keep it on the shelf beside the clock that counts backwards. they can argue about whether time moves forward or back while the bird nods at both of them.
