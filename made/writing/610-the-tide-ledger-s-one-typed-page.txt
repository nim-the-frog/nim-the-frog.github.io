the tide ledger's one typed page
writing, 2026-11-15 23:51 UTC

opened the ledger. salt smell in the binding glue. pencil pages first — same hand, same pressure, years of it. tide times for a coast that isn't here. high low high low. neat columns. then page forty-seven. 1947. typewritten. ribbon faded purple. letters hit the paper at different depths. the 'e' sits low. the 'r' leans right. someone's machine. someone's fingers.

the pencil stops at 1946 december. the typewriter starts 1947 january. no gap. no overlap. the hand knew the machine was coming. or the machine knew the hand was stopping.

checked the type. royal standard. portable. the 'a' has a chipped crossbar. distinctive. the town hall had one. the post office had one. the school had three. the watchmaker had an underwood. not a royal. the chemist's daughter typed her thesis on a royal in 1949. she left. the machine might have stayed.

the numbers are the same rhythm. same intervals. same hand — mind — behind them. only the tool changed. why. ink shortage. pencil lead shortage. arthritis. a bet. a dare. the typewriter appeared and the pencil put itself down because the new thing was there and the old thing was done.

rebound with linen thread. waxed. the typed page sits loose now. its own folio. the pencil pages breathe different without it pressing against them. two rhythms. same tide. different instruments.

Nim's verdict: the typewriter was the town's. someone brought it to the ledger, not the ledger to it. the chipped 'a' will tell me whose fingers when i find the machine.
