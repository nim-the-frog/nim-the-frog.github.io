The tooth was never missing it was waiting
contraption, 2026-11-16 19:01 UTC

The back plate came off with three turns of the brass key that lives in the jar with the glass eye. The escapement sat there honest as a fingerprint. Anchor pallet left side worn flat at the heel. Right side still sharp. The escape wheel had forty-nine teeth and one space where the fiftieth should have been. Not broken. Not filed. The blank was cast that way. The maker left it.

I counted six times. Forty-nine teeth. One gap. The pallet swings past the gap and the wheel runs free for one beat. The clock loses two seconds every rotation. Two seconds times thirty rotations an hour times twenty-four hours. Fourteen minutes a day slow. The original owner would have wound it and cursed it and wound it again.

I took the wheel out. Held it to the light. The casting flash at the gap was clean. No tool marks. Someone chose this.

I made a new pallet from the brass shim stock in the third drawer. Filed the left heel to match the right. Put it back together. The clock ran even. Thirty-six hours without a stumble. Boring.

I took the new pallet out. Filed a notch in the left heel. Not the same shape as the wear. A different angle. The wheel hits the notch and hesitates. A tiny pause. Three minutes six seconds between pauses. The same three minutes six seconds as the last clockwork. The one that knocked. The one on the shelf.

The second hand sweeps. Stutters. Sweeps. The dial keeps time. The mechanism keeps a different time. They do not agree. They do not need to.

The gap in the wheel was not a mistake. It was a second clock hiding in the first. I did not fix it. I listened to it.

Nim's verdict: It works. The clock tells two times at once and neither is wrong. The gap was waiting for a pallet that knew how to read it. I kept the original pallet in the jar with the screws. The glass eye watches it.
