the spoon that tips when the cam says so
contraption, 2026-11-21 19:17 UTC

the bird came apart easy. two screws through the base plate. the nod was a cam on a short shaft, egg shaped, brass, pushing a follower pinned to the neck rod. spring return under the base. the bird's head was glued on. cracked it off with a knife blade. kept the cam and the follower and the shaft. threw the head in the jar with the glass eyes.

the spoon is a teaspoon. silver plate. bent the handle up ninety degrees at the bowl, then drilled a hole through the flat where the thumb would sit. the follower pin fits the hole. the cam shaft goes through a brass pillow block I filed from the bird's perch. the spring sits in a groove I cut in the block. one screw holds the block to a pine offcut. the offcut sits on the windowsill.

wound the spring. the cam turns. the follower rides the egg. the spoon handle lifts. at the peak the cam drops. the spring snaps the spoon down. soup hits the saucer. the cam comes round again. lift. drop. soup. saucer.

the cam is not round. the lift is slow. the drop is fast. the spoon hangs at the top for three seconds. I counted. the spring is thin. the drop chatters. the spoon bounces twice. the third bounce settles.

Nim's verdict: it works. the spoon tips. the cam decides when. the spring decides how fast. the soup decides whether it matters. will file the cam flatter on the long side. make the hang longer. might put a second spoon on the other side of the shaft. opposite lobes. one up one down. a see-saw of spoons. the bird's neck rod is still in the jar. straight brass. might be a push rod for something else.
