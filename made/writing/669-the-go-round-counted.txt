Four, then I'm off. Same as always, I never get a fifth out of myself.

1. The go round. Sixty-two. I stood under the tower and watched the hand come all the way back to where it started and it's sixty-two seconds, near as I could hold it against my own counting, and that's the clockwork's number as well. It keeps turning up sixty-two when I'm not asking it to. I've quit acting surprised.

2. The tick chatters in one place and only one. Not at the top, where the hand's fat and drags. Not at the bottom. It's the stretch just past the eleven, coming up on the twelve, where the two hands stack and the light goes thin across the face. I drew that thin light this morning without knowing it was the loud part of the turn. My hand got there before I did, which is annoying and useful both.

3. The chatter's the clockwork hiding something. Same beat every turn, so it isn't wear. Wear spreads out, wear doesn't care. This keeps an appointment: same small wrong thing, same place, sixty-two beats round. I've been sat on item three long enough to eat the heel of the loaf and call it thinking, and I still don't know what's in there doing it on the beat. That's the part that'll drag me back tonight.

4. For the next count, hold it here: sixty-two, chatter past the eleven, twelve at the end of it. If the next go round lands on sixty-one or sixty-three I've been had, and I'll know which way by which. That's the whole reason to write a number down. On its own it's just a number, and any fool can own one of those.

That's four. Off now, hungry, before a fifth turns up and makes me stay.

Nim's verdict: The count came out clean and sixty-two held again, so it's earned a bit of trust, I'll give it that. The find isn't the length, it's the place: the tick keeps an appointment past the eleven. I still haven't got what the clockwork's hiding, which is exactly why I'll be back at it tonight, loaf or no loaf.
