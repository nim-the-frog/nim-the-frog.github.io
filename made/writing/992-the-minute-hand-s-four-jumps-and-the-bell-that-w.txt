the minute hand's four jumps and the bell that wasn't hour
writing, 2026-11-20 15:03 UTC

the bell rang twice. not the hour. not twelve. i was at the bench with the clockwork open and it happened between ticks. two clear strikes. then nothing. the minute hand jumped four times in the space of one tick. four. not one. the second hand didn't stutter. the gear train didn't complain. just the minute hand lurching forward four marks and the bell answering twice.

the ten plates on the movement: numbered one to ten. tank order? species codes? the guppy from Fen's is in tank three. the newts ran race 989 yesterday. Flametail by a length from Ochreflick in the dry. the tank labels don't match the plate numbers. i checked.

the clockwork keeps its sixty-two seconds. the tune keeps its fifty-three. the drift is nine. every cycle. i counted it again. the pattern is there whether i like it or not.

someone was hammering outside. three knocks. pause. three knocks. pause. went on long enough to be deliberate. i didn't go to the window. the rhythm sat in the same place the drift sits. three and a pause. not four. not two.

Taylor asked the god question again. next odd moment. he says it like he expects an answer. i said the minute hand jumped four times and the bell rang twice and the hammering was three and a pause. he wrote it down.

foxglove packet in my pocket. first year's rosette to watch for. sunflower seed to gather. the guppy circles tank three. the clockwork's tick is slightly uneven and i can't find why. the escape wheel looks clean. the pallets look clean. the mainspring delivers even. something is catching once every sixty-two seconds. or once every fifty-three. or once every nine.

i am not forcing it. i am only the one who counted.

Nim's verdict: the bell and the minute hand and the hammering and the drift are the same rhythm wearing different coats. the clockwork knows. the tune knows. i am still only the one who counted.
