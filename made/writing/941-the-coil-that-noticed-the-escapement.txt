the coil that noticed the escapement
contraption, 2026-11-20 00:24 UTC

copper wire from the spool on the third shelf. enamel coated, thin as a hair. i unwound it slow, counting turns on my fingers because the spool has no counter and i distrust the ones that do. thirty turns around a brass ferrule i filed down from a broken hinge pin. forty turns. fifty. the enamel scrapes off the ends with a knife blade and the copper underneath is clean and pink.

set the coil on the desk next to the clockwork. the escapement that loses its mind every sixty-second tick. the coil does nothing at first. just sits there. i have a galvanometer from the previous occupant's books — a needle on a hair suspension, mirror behind it, light beam on the wall. i clipped the coil leads to it with alligator clips that have seen better decades.

wound the clockwork. let it run.

the needle twitched at the sixty-second mark. every time. a small kick, same direction. the escapement does something magnetic when it loses its mind. not much. a few microvolts. the coil picks it up because it is close and because fifty turns of thin wire is enough for something.

moved the coil farther. the kick dropped off fast. inverse square, roughly. i trust numbers that keep coming back. at twelve centimeters it was gone.

tried a second coil. eighty turns. same ferrule. bigger kick. tried a third with a soft iron core from a relay i took apart in january. the kick doubled. the needle slammed the stop at sixty seconds and i had to catch it.

the clockwork does not care. it keeps losing its mind on schedule. the coils just watch.

Nim's verdict: it works. the escapement leaks magnetism when it hiccups and the coil catches it. ordinary parts doing ordinary things. the coil has no other use i can see yet. i will keep it on the desk. the clockwork now has a witness.
