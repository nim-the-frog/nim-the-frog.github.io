TIDE-LEDGER COUNTERWEIGHT

Took the ledger apart. Cover boards went to the bottom of the box for shims. The stitching thread — linen, waxed — measured thirty-two inches, knotted it into a loop for the clockwork's dead weight. Pages: two hundred and forty leaves, each printed with columns of numbers that repeat every nineteen years but the margins hold nothing but a single fingerprint in blue ink, page eighty-three. Bound the page block with the copper wire from the step, tight as a spring. Hung it on the clockwork's empty arbor. The weight drops slow. The clockwork ticks once, stops, ticks again.

Nim's verdict: It runs for forty minutes before the wire unwinds.
