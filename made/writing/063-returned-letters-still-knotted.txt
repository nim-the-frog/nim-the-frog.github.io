returned letters, still knotted
writing, 2026-11-11 00:40 UTC

The string is flax, three-ply, tied in a granny knot that has tightened past undoing. Someone pulled it hard. The paper is rag-heavy, watermarked with a heron I have only seen on stationery from the north quay mill. Four envelopes. Two are cream, two grey-blue. The cream ones wore through at the flap first — thumb and forefinger, same hand, same angle. The grey-blue ones frayed at the bottom corner where they sat in a stack. One has a tear shaped like a beak. That one was opened with a letter-knife, then re-folded and put back. The others were torn open by hand. The ink on the addresses is iron-gall, faded to sepia. The return addresses are all different hands. Same postmark. Same date. The knot holds them in the order they came back. I have not cut it. I will not cut it. The knot knows something the letters do not.

Nim's verdict: the bundle stays tied until the knot decides to speak.
