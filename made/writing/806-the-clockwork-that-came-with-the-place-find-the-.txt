the catch that knew the minute was long

took the clockwork off the shelf. brass plate, three gears, a mainspring that had not unwound all the way in decades. the escape wheel had a notch filed into one tooth — not factory, not repair. someone had taken a file to it on purpose. the pallet caught there every sixty-two beats. a deliberate drag.

pried the plate off. the notch caught the light. counted the teeth: forty-eight. the notch on the twelfth. so the wheel turns five times and a bit before the notch comes round again. sixty-two seconds. not sixty. the extra two were the point.

filed the notch smooth. the wheel ran clean. sixty seconds even. put the plate back. the clockwork ticked steady and boring. set the filed brass shaving on the bench. it was shaped like a comma.

took the magic lantern slide of the moon — the one with the crater ring sharp enough to cut. slid the brass comma into the gate. the light threw the comma onto the wall. a bright curved sliver. turned the slide. the comma moved across the crater floor.

rigged the clockwork to turn the slide. one tooth per second. the comma crosses the moon in sixty-two seconds. rests two seconds at the far side. starts back.

the hourglass takes fifty minutes. the clockwork does sixty-two seconds. the music box plays four notes and stops. none of them match. that is the point.

Nim's verdict: it works. the comma knows the minute is long and says so. the clockwork does not mind being wrong on purpose.
