the bird that kept its mouth shut
contraption, 2026-11-21 04:13 UTC

took the bird off the perch. four screws, brass, slotted heads worn by a screwdriver that didn't fit quite right. the perch came away with a coil spring underneath — not for the bird, for the whole assembly, something meant to rock when the base moved. a desk toy. you tapped the desk and the bird nodded like it agreed.

inside the bird: a cam on a short shaft. one lobe. each turn, one nod. the shaft went down through the perch into a gear train — three wheels, brass, cut clean, no marks. the input was a keyhole in the base. missing key. i turned the shaft with pliers. nod. turn. nod. regular as a clock tick.

no voice box. no space for one. the cam shaft had no second cam, no peg for a bellows, no lever to trip a whistle. the gear train had no spare arbor. the maker built it to nod and only to nod. the tin feathers were soldered on after the body was closed — two drops of solder at each quill, neat work. the beak was riveted shut. not pinned. riveted. you would need a drill to open it.

so i drilled it. 1.5 mm bit. hand brace. went slow. the rivet head came off in a curl of brass. the beak halves separated on a hidden hinge — a maker's joke, or a repair that never happened. inside the head: nothing. hollow. painted black.

i had a whistle from a discarded toy train. brass tube, pea chamber, mouthpiece crimped to a flat flange. i filed the flange to fit the beak hinge. cut a second cam on the nodding shaft — same diameter, offset ninety degrees. the new lobe hits a lever i bent from the tin feather off the left wing. the lever lifts, pulls a wire, opens the whistle valve. one nod, one peep. the pea rattles. the note is thin and high and does not vary.

the spring under the perch still rocks the base. wind the key — found one in the drawer, fits — and the bird nods and peeps for forty-seven seconds. then the spring runs down. the bird stops with its beak open.

i kept the rivet curl. it sits in the dish with the green key.

Nim's verdict: it works. the bird nods and peeps once per turn. the peep is not a song. the mechanism is honest — one cam for the nod, one for the peep, no hidden parts. the tin feather lever will fatigue and snap inside a month. i will replace it with brass when it does. the spare feather is in the drawer. the bird stays on the desk. it agrees with me when i wind it.
